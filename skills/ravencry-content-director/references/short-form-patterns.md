# Short-form patterns

Use these patterns as structures, not copy. Tie every claim to the actual recording.

## Attention-reset hooks

Before choosing a pattern, name the exchange: what useful ability, clarity, or avoided mistake does the viewer receive in this video? A hook earns attention when it makes that payoff feel both desirable and unexpectedly close.

| Pattern | Formula | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False finish | "This looks finished. It fails the moment [specific condition]." | Reliability and edge cases. |
| Reversed cause | "[Visible problem] is not caused by [obvious cause]. It starts earlier." | Debugging and architecture. |
| Small action, large consequence | "This one-line choice decides whether [concrete outcome]." | Workflows and design decisions. |
| Old belief versus proof | "I thought [common belief] mattered—until this test showed [surprise]." | Experiments and failures. |
| Borrowable advantage | "If you build [system], steal this before it costs you [specific failure]." | Tutorials and tooling. |

Avoid empty hooks such as "watch me code," "big update," or "we are building something exciting." They make the viewer wait to discover the benefit. Also avoid surprise with no exchange: the viewer should receive the promised insight before the first 10 seconds end.

## Light and dark, without manipulation

Use contrast to make the lesson matter:

- **Dark:** name the costly false assumption or failure mode the viewer recognizes.
- **Light:** show the new capability, relief, or control that the technique creates.
- **Proof:** demonstrate the mechanism, fixture, or result that connects the two.

Frame the dark side as a real engineering consequence, not catastrophe language. Do not claim a transformation that the recording cannot prove.

## Narration rules

- Make a claim, show why it matters, then demonstrate it.
- Prefer short active sentences and concrete nouns: "queued mutation," not "offline functionality."
- Name the trade-off. Credible uncertainty is more interesting than a polished victory lap.
- Cut setup that does not change the viewer's understanding.
- Say exactly what to remember in the final line.

## Platform adaptation

Keep the core narration intact. Change the framing only after the script works.

| Platform | Adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| X | Lead with the sharpest claim; use the post text to state the practical takeaway. |
| TikTok | Keep the opening visually immediate and conversational; use native captions. |
| YouTube Shorts | Use a searchable, specific title that says what the technique solves. |
| LinkedIn | Frame the lesson as a professional engineering decision, not a trend. |

## Ending patterns

- "The feature was easy. Making it fail safely was the actual work."
- "That one constraint changed the whole implementation."
- "Next I am testing what happens when [specific unresolved edge case]."

Use a question only when the answer genuinely invites developer experience: "What is the smallest test that has caught your biggest production bug?"
